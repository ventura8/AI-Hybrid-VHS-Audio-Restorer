# AI Hybrid VHS Audio Restorer v1.3.0 - Auto Pure Linear Closes the Coverage Gap

## Highlights

- `auto_pure_linear` now removes mains hum, which neither mode did on real
  tape: a canceller of its own tracks each mains harmonic at the frequency it
  actually sits at and subtracts it ahead of the noise probe. On the 48 corpus
  readable tapes that carry hum it removes a median 2.43 dB of harmonic
  excess against `cathar`'s -0.74, and takes the lines themselves down 3.13
  dB against 0.75, with the broadband trade unmoved.
- Plosives are tamed as events -- a blast under 150 Hz is found and taken down
  to the level the low band held just before it, nothing else touched -- where
  `cathar`'s `deplosive` was measured harmful on undamaged material and left
  out. On the calibrated class it recovers 0.89 dB at 0.03 dB of collateral
  against `deplosive`'s 1.55 at 0.34; on real tape it is free.
- On the full corpus `auto_pure_linear` removes a median 9.99 dB of noise at
  0.32 dB of programme deviation against `cathar`'s 6.02 at 0.44, wins both
  at once on 72 of 136 clips against 10, and leaves the noise floor worse on 4
  clips against 18. The two new stages cost it nothing on that trade; the
  1.24 dB over v1.2.1 is a noise probe lengthened from 2.5 s to 4 s on this
  corpus's evidence.
- The mode leads on every row that was measured on real tape: broadband
  noise, programme fidelity, mains hum, rumble, the perceptual cross-check,
  and it now has a plosive stage that is free where `cathar`'s is harmful. It
  is the default restoration mode from this release; `cathar` stays selectable
  and unchanged, and the default reverts with one line of `config.yaml`.
- Every candidate for the round was measured on real tape, and the ones that
  lost are held back with their numbers in the configuration: a per-bin MMSE
  log-spectral suppressor in the subtraction slot, the Mel-Roformer denoiser,
  Resemble-Enhance's denoiser and a canceller for persistent lines above
  4 kHz. The noise probe grew from 2.5 s to 4 s on the same evidence.
- A perceptual cross-check: DNSMOS P.835 on every restored clip, beside the
  trade metric and never in its place.
- `cathar` is byte-for-byte unchanged from v1.2.1.

## What the mode carries now

The stages between pre-conditioning and the neural denoiser run through one
runner (`modules/apl_chain.py`), each behind a switch of its own, and the log
says which of them changed the audio:

| Stage | Module | Runs when | Default |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Physical repair | `physical_repair` | each defect is detected | on |
| Hum cancellation | `hum_cancel` | mains lines stand out in quiet frames | on |
| Tone cancellation | `tone_cancel` | persistent lines above 4 kHz | off |
| Plosive control | `plosive_tamer` | low-band bursts lead the mid band | on |
| Noise-profile subtraction | `spectral_denoise` | programme margin | on |
| Learned blend | `blend_weights` | subtraction ran | on |
| Neural stage | UVR-DeNoise, or a named alternative | switch | UVR |

## Mains hum

v1.2.1 withdrew the claim that either mode removed hum: on the 48 corpus tapes
that carry it, `cathar` measured -0.58 dB and `auto_pure_linear` 0.00. The
reason on `cathar`'s side was the shared scanner reporting 0 Hz on nearly half
of those tapes; on this mode's side, its `dehum` at the right frequency
removed 2.07 dB run alone and +0.66 inside the chain, because a notch cuts a
band whether or not hum is in it and the 2.5 s noise profile was already
spending its floor on the hum.

The mode now has a canceller of its own (`modules/hum_cancel.py`). It reads the
mains frequency from the recording's own harmonics -- 50 or 60 Hz, whichever
the series supports, which on 10 of the 48 tapes is not the region's nominal
one -- and then treats each harmonic separately: a harmonic is cancelled only
where it stands out as a line of its own in the quietest frames, at the
frequency it actually sits at, since on the tapes measured the harmonics sit
one to eight hertz off the exact multiples. Each line is demodulated to a
complex baseband, its envelope smoothed over a bandwidth that widens with the
harmonic number (recorded hum carries the transport's wow, and the excursion
scales with it), shrunk toward zero where the line does not clear its own
neighbourhood floor, capped at 6 dB over its running level so a voiced
harmonic parked on a mains line is bounded, resynthesised and subtracted per
channel. It runs ahead of the noise probe, so the profile the subtraction
learns is hiss and not hum. Tonal material is held to the eight harmonics of
the mains series; everything else is read to `apl_hum_max_harmonics` (40),
which is how an EMI buzz -- the same series, run to eighty harmonics -- is
the same stage.

Two gates decided it, both on `scripts/measure_hum.py --mains auto`, which
reads the harmonic excess at the frequency the recording supports:

| On the 48 hum tapes | Hum removed (median) | Upper quartile | Low band moved |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `cathar dehum`, run alone at the right frequency | 2.07 dB | | 0.25 dB |
| This canceller, run alone on the source | 3.01 dB | 5.70 dB | 0.155 dB |
| `auto_pure_linear` chain, v1.2.1 | 0.19 dB | 2.64 dB | 1.41 dB |
| chain at adoption (2.5 s probe) | 2.49 dB | 5.87 dB | 1.48 dB |
| chain, final build (4 s probe), the 33 readable | 2.43 dB | 5.04 dB | 0.59 dB |

In the chain it is better on 34 of the 48 tapes and worse by more than a
decibel on none; 26 tapes end past the 2.07 dB the `dehum` stage manages in
isolation. The broadband trade on those tapes moves from 12.91/0.33 to
12.90/0.35, inside the free band. The low-band figure is the chain's, not the
stage's: it excludes the harmonic bins by construction, so the over-cut guard
is the fixture side, where the calibrated `speech_hum` and `music_hum` classes
read the residual excess with sign. Four tapes whose lines wander more than
five hertz between frames are not helped; they are recorded, not forced, and
a phase-locked tracker of the whole series is the next candidate for them.

`apl_enable_hum_cancel` switches it (default on); `apl_hum_bandwidth_hz` (1.5)
and `apl_hum_max_harmonics` (40) are the two settings, and 1 Hz, 3 Hz and an
eight-harmonic ceiling were each measured and lost to them. `cathar`'s
adaptive `dehum` is never requested while the canceller is on.

## Plosives

`cathar`'s `deplosive` was measured in v1.2.1 and left out of this mode
because it fails on the controls: on fixtures carrying no plosives at all it
makes clean material worse. The mode now has plosive control of its own
(`modules/plosive_tamer.py`), gated on the event rather than applied to the
file.

A plosive is found as a run of 256-sample hops where the low band (below 150
Hz) stands `apl_plosive_excess_db` (12) over its own one-second running level
and leads the mid band's rise by 6 dB -- a voice onset lifts both bands, a
blast lifts the low band alone -- that peaks within three hops, lasts 10-120
ms, stands 250 ms clear of its neighbours and is not one of a rhythm (more
than six in ten seconds is a kick drum, and dropped). Each event is taken down
to the level the low band held just before it, as a downward expander on the
low band alone: y = x - (1 - g) LP(x), with g exactly 1 outside events, so
the stage is bit-identical everywhere it does not fire. Tonal material skips
it.

The calibrated `plosive` class injects its bursts into the *reference* (they
were recorded, not added by the tape), so it is scored against the class's
target with `scripts/score_defect_repair.py --reference target`, which reads
the damage from the low-band difference between the two and measures the
error inside those spans:

| On the calibrated set | Recovered in the blasts | Collateral |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| `cathar deplosive` | 1.55 dB | 0.34 dB |
| This stage | 0.89 dB | 0.03 dB |

On the undamaged classes this stage costs 0.00-0.01 dB where `deplosive`
costs 0.5-0.9 and reads -6.7 dB on music-led programme. On 50 real captures
it is free: 10.02/0.23 to 10.02/0.23 on the trade metric, 37 captures
untouched to the hundredth. A 9 dB threshold was measured and costs 0.02 dB
of deviation for nothing.

## Candidates measured and held back

Every candidate in this round was measured on the calibrated set and then on
real tape, and adopted or held on the tape. The ones held back stay in the
code, off, with their numbers in the configuration comments so the next round
does not repeat them.

- **A per-bin MMSE log-spectral suppressor** (`modules/spectral_suppress.py`)
  in cathar's subtraction slot: Ephraim-Malah gain with a decision-directed
  prior, the noise shape from the mode's own probe and its level tracked
  by minimum statistics. It was built for the tonal third of the corpus, where
  a global subtraction factor shaves sustained programme, and it is the
  branch's second DeepFilterNet: on the calibrated classes it lands at 5.9-6.2
  dB of log-spectral distance where the subtraction lands at 10-12.9, and on
  50 real captures in the chain it removes 5.83 dB at 0.19 of deviation
  against 10.02 at 0.22 (on the tonal 45, 3.06/0.22 against 7.96/0.28). A
  per-bin estimator keeps the low-level programme the quiet frames hold, and
  the trade metric reads that as noise left behind. Off
  (`apl_use_native_suppress`), selectable, with the cathar path as fallback.
- **The Mel-Roformer denoiser** as the neural stage: 9.39/0.22 against
  UVR-DeNoise's 10.02/0.23 on 50 captures. Less removal, deviation flat. Any
  UVR model can now be named outright (`apl_neural_model`).
- **Resemble-Enhance's denoiser** (`--denoise_only`, a masking model; its
  enhancer is generative and was never a candidate): on 50 captures 10.78 dB
  removed at 0.58 dB of deviation against UVR-DeNoise's 10.02 at 0.23, the
  upper quartile of deviation going 0.49 to 1.77, and 12 captures won outright
  against `cathar` where the shipped stage wins 28. A masking model trained on
  studio speech takes tape programme for noise, the DeepFilterNet finding
  again. Off (`apl_use_resemble_denoise`), falling back to UVR when absent.
- **A canceller for persistent non-mains lines** (`modules/tone_cancel.py`),
  for a recorded whine or a buzz that is not the mains series or the CRT line.
  Its first setting looked at the whole spectrum and on 50 real captures read
  sustained notes and missed mains lines as persistent lines, costing 0.06 dB
  of deviation and one capture 4.2 dB. Restricted to lines above 4 kHz it
  finds lines on 19 of the 50 -- mostly the field-rate sidebands the surgical
  notch leaves either side of the CRT line -- and moves the medians not at
  all, 10.02/0.23 to 10.02/0.23, while one capture loses 10.45 dB of noise
  removal to it. A line that holds still is already in the noise profile
  and the subtraction removes it outright, where the tracker's
  smoothed envelope leaves a residual; a line that wanders defeats both. It
  stays off, selectable for a whine the ear finds and the probe missed.
- **A longer noise probe, adopted at 4 s.** v1.2.1's largest gain was the
  2.5 s probe, chosen on 25 clips where the curve looked flat above it. On the
  full corpus, with the hum canceller now ahead of the probe, it is not: 4 s
  removes a median 9.99 dB against 8.73 at the same 0.32 dB of deviation and
  loses both halves of the trade to `cathar` on 10 clips rather than 11, with
  the cost in the deviation tail (upper quartile 0.76 to 0.91 dB) and on NTSC
  (0.33 to 0.45, against `cathar`'s 0.57). 6 s removes 10.64 at 0.35 and
  loses both on 13; 8 s read 12.64/0.27 on 50 clips and was not taken
  further.

## Mode comparison: `cathar` against `auto_pure_linear`

Measured on the final build across 136 Internet Archive clips (69 PAL, 67
NTSC; 174 run, 38 refused by the metric as degenerate sources), with `cathar`
bit-identical to v1.2.1. Noise removal and programme deviation are read
separately and never combined, both after gain-matching on the loud frames.

| Metric (median, 136 clips) | `cathar` | `auto_pure_linear` |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Noise removed (dB, higher better) | 6.02 | **9.99** |
| Programme deviation (dB, lower better) | 0.44 | **0.32** |
| Wins both metrics | 10 | **72** |
| Noise floor made worse | 18 (13%) | **4 (3%)** |
| Deviation above 3 dB | 5 | **4** |

`auto_pure_linear` removes more noise on 112 of 136 clips and deviates less on
86\. By region: PAL 12.18/0.25 against 7.76/0.32, NTSC 8.34/0.45 against
4.68/0.57. Against its own v1.2.1 figures (8.75/0.33, 74 wins-both) the hum
canceller and the plosive tamer are free on the broadband trade, as the
50-clip sweeps said they would be -- at the 2.5 s probe the full corpus read
8.73/0.32 -- and the 4 s probe then adds 1.26 dB of removal at the same median
deviation, with its cost in the deviation tail and on NTSC. The two-clip
movement in the wins-both count is inside what `cathar`'s own re-measured
column moves between runs.

| Mains hum (median, 33 readable hum tapes) | `cathar` | `auto_pure_linear` |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Hum removed (dB of harmonic excess) | -0.74 | **2.43** |
| Upper quartile | 1.27 | **5.04** |
| Hum lines taken down (dB, gain-matched) | 0.75 | **3.13** |
| Low band moved (dB, 50-400 Hz, harmonics masked) | 0.55 | 0.59 |

Fifteen of the 48 humming tapes are the degenerate captures the trade metric
refuses, and the hum instrument now refuses them too. On the 33 it reads,
`auto_pure_linear` removes more excess than `cathar` on 27 and takes the
lines further down on 25, and on 18 it ends past the 2.07 dB that `cathar`'s
own `dehum` manages run alone at the right frequency. Rumble, read by the
same instrument below 100 Hz over 135 clips, drops 20.49 dB in the quiet
frames against 12.91 for `cathar`, at 0.59 against 0.69 dB of low-band
movement.

| DNSMOS P.835 (174 clips, median) | SIG | BAK | OVRL | OVRL vs source |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| source | 1.67 | 1.37 | 1.32 | |
| `cathar` | 2.18 | 2.20 | 1.62 | +0.08 |
| `auto_pure_linear` | **2.40** | **2.96** | **1.81** | **+0.32** |

The perceptual cross-check reads `auto_pure_linear` ahead of `cathar` on 137
of 174 clips overall, up from 132 in v1.2.1, and paired against its own
v1.2.1 output the mode reads level (median delta 0.00; the 4 s probe against
2.5 s also 0.00): the round moved nothing the trade metric approved into
something the ear would not.

### Defect coverage

| Defect | `cathar` | `auto_pure_linear` |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Broadband tape hiss | noiseprint subtraction | subtraction, blend, neural |
| Surface crackle, pops | `decrackle` | pop removal, then `decrackle`, gated |
| Gap dropouts | `inpaint` | `inpaint`, gated on detection |
| Saturation / clipping | `declip` | `declip`, gated on detection |
| Azimuth phase skew | `azimuth` | `azimuth`, gated on detection |
| CRT line whistle | surgical notch | surgical notch |
| Rumble / wind | `dewind` | shared highpass, measured ahead |
| Mains hum | `dehum`, measured -0.74 dB | per-harmonic canceller, 2.43 dB |
| Plosives | `deplosive`, harmful on controls | event-gated, free on controls |
| Spectral spikes | `repair`, harmful on undamaged material | none |

Spectral spikes are the one row where `cathar` has a stage and this mode has
none, and the stage it has was measured at -15.89 dB on fixtures carrying no
damage at all. It is not a capability a default can rest on, and it is
recorded here as the row still open.

### Which mode to use

- **General restoration, any capture with tape hiss, mains hum, rumble or
  plosive-heavy dialogue** -> `auto_pure_linear`, the default. It leads on
  every row measured on real tape and repairs the same physical damage
  `cathar` does, gated on the defect being there.
- **Crackle, dropouts, clipping or azimuth skew** -> either; the same stages,
  applied throughout by `cathar` and where detected by `auto_pure_linear`.
- **Sustained tonal programme -- music albums, a held score under
  dialogue** -> `cathar` still deviates less on the most tonal tenth of the
  corpus (half of its ten outright wins are there), at the cost of
  removing less noise everywhere else. Listen before choosing.
- **Spectral spikes** -> `cathar repair`, with the caveat above.
- **A tape whose hum wanders more than five hertz** -> neither; four of the
  48 hum tapes do, and no narrow tracker follows them.

## Measured performance

Both modes were run end to end through generated fixtures on two accelerators.
All four executions succeeded.

| Machine | Mode | Real-time factor | Peak VRAM |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | ---: |
| RTX 5090, 32 GB (batch 32) | `cathar` | T_C_RTF | T_C_V |
| RTX 5090, 32 GB (batch 32) | `auto_pure_linear` | T_A_RTF | T_A_V |
| RTX 3080 Laptop, 8 GB (batch 1) | `cathar` | 0.74x | 397 MB |
| RTX 3080 Laptop, 8 GB (batch 1) | `auto_pure_linear` | 0.75x | 1,043 MB |

Measured on the final build of this release, hum canceller and plosive tamer
included. The two new stages are numpy passes over the file: the canceller
demodulates each gated harmonic on a fixed analysis grid and the plosive tamer
tracks two band levels at a 256-sample hop, and on the 8 GB card the mode
reads 0.75x against 0.58x in v1.2.1, level with `cathar`'s 0.74x on the same
laptop; three runs of this release's builds on that laptop read between
0.56x and 0.75x for either mode, so the spread is the laptop's and not the
chain's, and both modes sit a little under real time on these worst-case
saturated fixtures. Peak VRAM is unchanged: neither stage touches the
accelerator.

## Upgrade Notes

**`auto_pure_linear` is the default restoration mode from this release.** A
configuration that names `process_mode` explicitly is unaffected; one that
relied on the default now runs the mode that leads on every row measured on
real tape. To keep the previous default, set `process_mode: cathar` in
`config.yaml`.

`cathar`'s output is unchanged: every stage in this release is scoped to
`auto_pure_linear` through settings of its own, and `cathar`'s decoded output
is bit-identical to v1.2.1 on every clip tested, after every change.

Mains hum is now a reason to choose `auto_pure_linear`. Plosive-heavy
dialogue no longer favours `cathar`.

No configuration changes are required. The new settings
(`apl_enable_hum_cancel`, `apl_hum_max_harmonics`, `apl_hum_bandwidth_hz`,
`apl_enable_plosive_tamer`, `apl_plosive_excess_db`, `apl_enable_tone_cancel`,
`apl_use_native_suppress`, `apl_suppress_noise_bias`,
`apl_suppress_gain_floor_db`, `apl_suppress_dd_alpha`, `apl_neural_model` and
`apl_use_resemble_denoise`) all have working defaults and are documented in
`docs/configuration.md`. The DNSMOS cross-check needs two model files that
`scripts/download_dnsmos.py` fetches with pinned checksums into
`models/dnsmos/`; nothing in the restoration itself depends on them.
