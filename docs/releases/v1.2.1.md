# AI Hybrid VHS Audio Restorer v1.2.1 - Auto Pure Linear Overtakes Cathar

## Highlights

- `auto_pure_linear` now removes more tape noise than `cathar` while disturbing
  the programme less, measured across 136 Internet Archive clips in both PAL and
  NTSC. In v1.2.0 it lost on both counts.
- Noise floor regressions in that mode fall from 47% of clips to 3%, and on
  NTSC from 52% to 4%.
- A noise probe of its own, at 2.5 s rather than the shared 0.75 s, which
  accounts for most of the improvement, and a subtraction factor gated on the
  material's tonality, which closed the one fidelity gap `cathar` still held.
- A learned per-bin blend that repairs spectral over-subtraction instead of
  trading fidelity against it, now with stationarity and neighbourhood features.
- Deterministic tonal cleanup is off by default in that mode, on ground-truth
  evidence that it removed programme along with the hum.
- `auto_pure_linear` now repairs physical tape damage as well: crackle,
  dropouts, saturation and azimuth skew, each gated on its own defect being
  detected. It previously repaired none of it.
- A fixture set calibrated to real tape, property by property, that agrees
  with all four real-tape verdicts the realism check runs, carries every fault
  in the defect catalog that a fixture can inject (sibilance and a constant
  speed error are the two it does not) and the ten the preservation literature
  adds, and reports which of them the scanner can see. The Internet Archive
  corpus behind the calibration grew from 192 clips of 192 tapes to 1,982
  clips of 847.
- DeepFilterNet3 as an opt-in neural stage, off by default because on real tape
  it triples programme deviation against UVR-DeNoise.
- `cathar` is byte-for-byte unchanged from v1.2.0.

## Physical damage repair

The mode removed noise well and repaired no physical damage at all. Closing that
by switching on the stages `cathar` runs is the approach already known to fail
here, so each was measured on its own against paired fixtures -- on the defect it
targets, and on material carrying none of it.

| Defect | Stage | Stepped damage | Calibrated damage |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Surface crackle, pops | `decrackle` | +10.00 dB | +1.19 dB |
| Gap dropouts | `inpaint` | +115.71 dB spectral | +0.43 dB |
| Saturation / clipping | `declip` | +10.03 dB | +4.86 dB |
| Azimuth phase skew | `azimuth` | +32.42 dB | +10.99 dB |

The stages were adopted on the first column and the second is what they do on
the calibrated fixture set that arrived later in this release: crackle drawn at
eight pops a second with bright tails rather than one fixed spike every half
second, dropouts at 5-50 ms with an abrupt edge in real tape noise rather than a
clean 50 ms hole in synthetic hiss, clipping and azimuth skew drawn from ranges.
`declip` and `azimuth` hold up. `inpaint` fills every realistic dropout, with
programme-like audio 13.5 dB under what was lost; the sample-domain figure is
small because the waveform is a reconstruction, not the original. `decrackle`
recovers about a tenth of what the stepped clicks said, and no setting of it,
of `declick` or of FFmpeg's `adeclick` does better on pops the size a tape
carries in the noise a tape carries them in. So the mode gained a pop stage of
its own, ahead of `decrackle`: a short autoregressive model per block, a pop
where its prediction residual stands seven robust scales out, and a
least-squares autoregressive refill of each span, left alone when other
outliers crowd it so a drum hit is not a pop. Measured at the true pop
positions of the calibrated crackle class it repairs +5.5 dB against
`decrackle`'s +1.7 and finds 98% of the pops; on undamaged speech it changes
40 dB under the programme; across 50 real captures it moves neither noise
removal nor deviation. `apl_depop_threshold` sets it, 0 switches it off.

Three further stages were measured and **not** adopted. `repair` and `deplosive`
fail on the controls rather than on their targets: on fixtures with no physical
damage at all they make clean material measurably worse, and they do on the
calibrated controls too. `declick` was dominated by `decrackle` on the stepped
clicks and does nothing at all on the calibrated crackle. All three remain in
`cathar`.

Gating each stage on its own defect is a correctness requirement, not an
optimisation: applied to everything, `decrackle` scores -11.09 dB on material
whose only defect is azimuth skew.

Across 174 real captures this is close to free: noise removal moves from 9.66 to
9.74 dB and programme deviation from 0.48 to 0.50. Set
`apl_enable_physical_repair: false` to switch it off.

## Mode comparison: `cathar` against `auto_pure_linear`

All figures are from the v1.2.1 build across 136 paired Internet Archive clips
(69 PAL, 67 NTSC) unless stated. 174 were run; 38 are excluded because the
source has under 3 dB between its quiet and loud frames -- a saturated or
constant-level track -- on which the metric measures nothing and had been
reporting 20-50 dB deviations identically for both modes. `cathar`'s figures
are unchanged from v1.2.0 because its output is bit-identical. Full methodology
is in `docs/cathar_vs_auto_pure_linear_1000_benchmark.md`.

### Chain composition

**`cathar`**: pre-condition, noiseprint, phase-coherent denoise, dehum, dewind,
declick, decrackle, inpaint, declip, repair, azimuth, mono-below, deplosive,
de-esser, enhance, sync, loudnorm. Applied as a fixed cascade.

**`auto_pure_linear`**: pre-condition, surgical notch, gated physical repair
(pop removal, decrackle, inpaint, declip, azimuth), noise-profile subtraction
(2.5 s probe, alpha 3.0 or 2.0 on tonal material), learned per-bin blend,
UVR-DeNoise, post-denoise cleanup, air shelf and expander, sync, loudnorm.
With `apl_use_deepfilternet` on, DeepFilterNet3 takes UVR-DeNoise's place
where it is installed; the chain is otherwise the same.

### Which mode to use

| Situation | Mode | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Tape hiss, general restoration | APL | More removal, less disturbance |
| Quiet or low-level captures | APL | Floor regressions 3% against 13% |
| NTSC material | APL | Largest lead of any segment |
| Crackle, dropouts, clipping, azimuth | either | Both repair these now |
| Strong mains hum | neither | v1.2.0 claim withdrawn, see notes |
| Spectral spikes, plosives | `cathar` | The two stages APL leaves out |
| Unknown or damaged tape | `cathar` | Broader cascade, and still the default |

`cathar` remains the default. Its defect coverage is broader and it is the safer
choice on unknown material; changing a released default is a decision for a
release, not for a benchmark.

## Why the numbers moved

The mode was learning its noise profile from 0.75 s of audio, which is too
little to estimate a noise floor from. It was not caught earlier because the
sweep meant to test it patched `modules/config.py` while `config.yaml` overrode
the value, so every attempt to change it reported no effect.

The second change is smaller and paid for the fidelity lead. Split by tonality,
the mode was disturbing the most tonal third of the corpus -- sustained music,
which sits near the noise profile -- by 0.49 dB against `cathar`'s 0.32, while
leading on the noisiest third. Subtracting at 2.0 rather than 3.0 on material
whose median spectral flatness is under 0.035 brings that third to 0.33, level
with `cathar`, and still removes more noise there. Corpus-wide it costs 1.2 dB
of the noise lead and buys 0.04 dB of deviation, moves the both-metrics wins
from 67 clips to 74, and takes the count of captures disturbed by more than
3 dB from eight to five, matching `cathar`. Set `apl_tonal_flatness_max: 0` to
switch the gate off.

DeepFilterNet3 was built from source and measured in the same chain as an
alternative neural stage. On paired synthetic fixtures it beat UVR-DeNoise by
3.52 dB of log-spectral distance; on 50 real captures it tripled programme
deviation, 0.22 to 0.66 dB, deviating more on 36 of them. It ships off, behind
`apl_use_deepfilternet`, and the fixtures that had it winning are the reason
the next section exists.

This release also replaces the metrics the previous benchmark ranked modes on.
Attenuation ratios rise whether a mode removed the defect or removed the
programme with it, and against clean references the stage those ratios rewarded
cut 6.7-10.7 dB below the truth in the hum harmonic bins. Noise removal and
programme damage are now measured separately and never combined.

## Measured performance

Both modes were run end to end through generated fixtures on two accelerators.
All four executions succeeded.

| Machine | Mode | Real-time factor | Peak VRAM |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | ---: |
| RTX 5090, 32 GB (batch 32) | `cathar` | 1.19x | 1,915 MB |
| RTX 5090, 32 GB (batch 32) | `auto_pure_linear` | 3.59x | 18,715 MB |
| RTX 3080 Laptop, 8 GB (batch 1) | `cathar` | 0.64x | 397 MB |
| RTX 3080 Laptop, 8 GB (batch 1) | `auto_pure_linear` | 0.58x | 1,043 MB |

Measured on the final build of this release, pop removal included. **These
fixtures are a worst case for `auto_pure_linear`, not a typical one.** They are
saturated, so the clipping repair runs, and that stage is the expensive one in
the chain: on an 8 s fixture `decrackle`, `azimuth` and `inpaint` take
0.02-0.03 s between them while `declip` takes 5.29 s, roughly 0.66x the audio
duration; pop removal adds about 0.1x. Across 25 real captures the clipping
detector fired on none of them, so ordinary tape does not pay for `declip`.

`auto_pure_linear` is otherwise the faster of the two on a large accelerator,
not the slower one as its extra subtraction and blending stages might suggest
-- `cathar` runs a longer cascade of DSP stages -- and on the 8 GB laptop card
the two are level, both a little under real time on this worst-case fixture.

Its peak VRAM scales with the batch size the hardware profile selects, so the
19 GB figure on the 5090 is opportunistic rather than required: the same mode
peaks at 1.0 GB on an 8 GB card. It does not need a large accelerator.

Every gate CI enforces was run locally at CI's exact scope before the final push,
and the release commit is green across all five CI jobs.

| mdformat, pymarkdown | clean |

Two gates were already failing on this branch before the work began -- the
per-file coverage minimum and ruff's import ordering -- and both are fixed. One
regression was introduced and fixed along the way: five added tests took a test
file from maintainability index 19.19 to 18.45 against a gate of 19, and they
now live in a file of their own.

**`cathar` regression gate.** The mode shipped in v1.2.0 and must not move. Its
decoded output is verified bit-identical to the pre-change reference on every
capture tested, by SHA256 rather than by any metric, after each change to the
chain; eight golden tests pin the shared pre-conditioning graph byte for byte.

**Measurement harness.** Four new pieces make this release's claims checkable:
a real-tape metric that reports noise removal and programme deviation
separately, paired fixtures carrying physical damage, a repair metric that
separates the fix from its collateral, and a fixture set calibrated to real
tape with a check that says whether it predicts it. All are in `scripts/` and
reproduce from `docs/cathar_vs_auto_pure_linear_1000_benchmark.md` and
`docs/validation.md`.

## Upgrade Notes

`cathar` remains the default restoration mode. Its output is unchanged: the
tuning in this release is scoped to `auto_pure_linear` through settings of its
own, and `cathar`'s decoded output is bit-identical to v1.2.0 on every clip
tested.

Consider `auto_pure_linear` for general restoration where tape hiss is the main
complaint. It now also repairs crackle, dropouts, clipping and azimuth skew,
which it did not in v1.2.0. Keep `cathar` for spectral spikes and plosive-heavy
dialogue -- the two stages that measured harmful on undamaged material and were
left out -- and as the safer choice on an unknown tape, where its broader
cascade covers more.

Mains hum is not a reason to choose either. v1.2.0 reported `cathar` leading
17.98x to 2.74x; measured on this build with harmonic excess across the 48
tapes that carry hum, `cathar` removes -0.58 dB and `auto_pure_linear` 0.00 dB.
`cathar`'s dehum runs at whatever frequency the scanner reports, and the
scanner reports 0 Hz when it misses. `auto_pure_linear` gains a corrected
detector and an `apl_enable_dehum` switch, off by default because the stage's
isolated gain (2.07 dB) does not survive the chain (+0.66 dB on a coin flip).

No configuration changes are required. The new settings
(`apl_enable_spectral_denoise`, `apl_noiseprint_duration_s`,
`apl_spectral_alpha`, `apl_spectral_alpha_tonal`, `apl_tonal_flatness_max`,
`apl_spectral_margin_db`, `apl_enable_learned_blend`,
`apl_enable_physical_repair`, `apl_depop_threshold`, the `apl_mute_*` dropout
thresholds, `apl_enable_dehum`, `apl_hum_min_excess_db`,
`apl_enable_tonal_cleanup` and `apl_use_deepfilternet`) all have working
defaults and are documented in `docs/configuration.md`.
